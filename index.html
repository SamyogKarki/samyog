About

Coming to security from the infrastructure side

I finished a BSc (Hons) in Computer Science in 2025, and for most of my final two years I was working at the same time — at Manakamana IT Solutions, as the person who set up office networks, administered user and administrator accounts across client sites, and looked after the company's own e-commerce platform.

Most of what I learned there was not about writing features. It was about the quieter questions: who should have an administrator account and who should not, what happens when a client's network stops routing, what breaks when someone is given more access than their job needs. Those are security questions, and I found I liked them more than the development work I was hired for.

Outside of work I run a small home lab — a Linux NAS with a media server on it, reachable from outside my house through a private Tailscale network rather than an open port. It is a modest setup, but building it is what taught me to think about exposure first and convenience second.

I am at the beginning of this, not the middle of it. What I have is a real foundation in networks, Linux and access control, and a deliberate plan to build security expertise on top of it.
